TidyShelf support & privacy

TidyShelf is a read-later extension for Chrome. It saves articles to read later in a clean reader, and keeps everything in your browser on your own device.

Get help Privacy policy

Get help

If something does not work the way you expected, write to me directly. One person answers this address, usually the same day and within two business days at the outside.

tkachenko.ya@icloud.com

What to include

  • Your Chrome version, from chrome://settings/help
  • The TidyShelf version, from chrome://extensions
  • What you expected to happen, and what happened instead
  • If one article saved badly, the address of that page (only if you are happy to share it, since it says what you were reading)

Two things worth checking first

  • If the save shortcut does nothing, another extension may already be using it. Choose a key for TidyShelf at chrome://extensions/shortcuts.
  • The text of a saved article is kept for offline reading, but its pictures still load from the original website, so they need an internet connection.

Privacy policy

TidyShelf does not collect any data.

No account, no server, no analytics, no ads.

There is no TidyShelf server. Everything you save is kept in your browser on this device, and none of it is ever sent to me or sold to anyone. The detail below is only there to say precisely what that means.

What is stored, and where

All of this is kept in the extension’s storage in your Chrome profile on this device:

  • Saved articles: the title, address, site name, a short excerpt, the date you saved it, whether you have read it, and your tags.
  • For articles saved with version 1.0.7 or later: the number of words, used to show the reading time.
  • For offline reading, when “Save full text” is on: the article’s text and a cleaned HTML copy of it.
  • Your highlights, and how far you have scrolled in each article.
  • Your settings: dark mode, the unread count on the toolbar icon, reader font size, the tag window, auto-mark as read, save full text, and the sort order of your list.
  • Whether you have answered the one-time request to leave a review.

Permissions, and why

  • Read and change all your data on all websites. A small script is present on each page, but it reads the article only when you save it: by clicking the TidyShelf button, pressing the save shortcut, or choosing “Save page to TidyShelf” from the right-click menu. Nothing is read at any other time.
  • tabs: to get the title and address of the tab you are saving.
  • activeTab and scripting: when you save a page that was open before TidyShelf was installed or updated, to load the article reader into that tab.
  • contextMenus (from version 1.0.7): to add “Save page to TidyShelf” to the menu that opens when you right-click a page.
  • storage and unlimitedStorage: to keep your library on your device without the default size limit.
  • notifications: to confirm a save made with the keyboard shortcut or the right-click menu, or to say why it failed.
  • favicon: to show website icons in your list, using Chrome’s built-in icon service.

The network

TidyShelf’s own code makes no network requests, and its fonts are bundled with the extension. Three things are worth knowing:

  • When you open a saved article, its pictures load from the original website, the same way they would if you visited it. That website can see your IP address and when the picture was requested.
  • Links open websites in a new tab: “Open original”, this policy, and “Write a review”, which opens TidyShelf’s page in the Chrome Web Store.
  • The welcome page shown after installation is part of the extension and loads nothing from the internet.

Export, import and deletion

  • Export creates a JSON file on your device. Import reads a file you choose. Neither is sent anywhere.
  • From version 1.0.7, deleting an article also removes its text, HTML copy, highlights and reading position: once the few seconds in which you can undo have passed (or, if you close the TidyShelf window sooner, the next time the browser starts or TidyShelf is updated).
  • Settings → “Clear all data” removes everything TidyShelf has stored. Uninstalling the extension removes it too.

Children

TidyShelf collects nothing from anyone, of any age.

Changes to this policy

If this ever changes, the date below changes with it, and the new version is posted at this address. Questions about it go to the same address as support.
